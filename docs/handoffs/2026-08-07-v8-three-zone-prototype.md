# Kickoff Clash V8 — three-zone prototype

Date: 2026-08-07
Branch: `agent/v8-three-zone-prototype`
Baseline: `agent/v7-chance-types-design`

## Purpose

This is an isolated prototype for the proposed Marvel Snap-style pivot. It does **not** replace V7 yet. V7 typed-chance work remains intact underneath the branch so the two models can be compared rather than conflated.

The design goal is a faster, more legible card battler. Football plausibility is useful as a sanity check, but **fun and decision quality are the primary calibration targets**.

## Locked core rules

- Match has four periods: `0–22`, `22–HT`, `HT–66`, `66–FT`.
- The selected XI is the player deck: exactly 11 player cards.
- Opening hand is 3 player cards plus the Manager, which is always available separately.
- Draw 2 player cards at the start of each period. By period four the player has seen all 11 players, assuming no draw manipulation.
- Board has three football zones: `DEF`, `MID`, `ATT`.
- Each zone holds at most 4 deployed players.
- `DEF` contributes player DEF only.
- `MID` contributes player ATT **and** DEF.
- `ATT` contributes player ATT only.
- Goals: **For every full +5 your ATT exceeds the opponent's DEF, score 1 goal.** The opponent resolves independently in the other direction.
- Current prototype banks those goals at the end of every period from the full persistent board.
- Draws are valid results.
- Playing out of position reduces **both** printed ATT and printed DEF:
  - natural zone: `0`
  - adjacent zone: `-2/-2`
  - two zones away: `-5/-5`
- Stats may go below zero.
- GK is a normal card in the XI and must be played from hand. GK is naturally a DEF card; there is no automatic keeper deployment.
- Actions use the simpler Snap-like timing model. `On Reveal` returns. Prototype vocabulary is `On Reveal`, `Ongoing`, triggered effects and `End of Period`.
- Cards do not move naturally after deployment. Individual cards can have `Moveable` status.
- The Manager is a one-shot transient card: pay normal Energy, play the Action into a chosen zone, resolve it, then the Manager disappears. Exact manager effects remain card-specific.
- Chance types survive the pivot as literal transient **Chance cards** added to hand. They cost Energy, resolve, disappear, and do not occupy one of the four zone slots.
- Match Conditions remain a symmetric match-level rule framework. Example: Slippery Pitch can temporarily reduce a random deployed stat each period.

## Chance-card sequencing assumption for the prototype

A Chance generated by an `On Reveal` player is created **after the current period's plays have been committed**, so it normally becomes playable from the next period onward.

That creates deliberate sequencing:

1. play the creator;
2. receive a typed Chance card;
3. hold it or spend Energy on it in a later period;
4. receiver / defender Actions can modify that Chance type.

This is preferable to creating a second hidden chance-resolution engine underneath the zone game.

## Calibration finding: V7 card economy cannot be copied directly

The strongest repeatable finding from rough simulation is not “football scores are too high”; it is that the **old card values and costs were authored for a different engine**.

In V8, a card's natural board contribution is:

- natural DEF card: `DEF`
- natural MID card: `ATT + DEF`
- natural ATT card: `ATT`

Call this **natural zone power**.

Example:

- 9 ATT winger in ATT = 9 zone power.
- 5 ATT / 4 DEF midfielder in MID = 9 zone power.
- 1 ATT / 9 DEF centre-back in DEF = 9 zone power.

This gives us a clean calibration axis. A midfielder should not cost the same as a specialist if its `ATT + DEF` natural contribution is materially larger.

A rough re-cost heuristic of `ceil(natural zone power / 2)` was enough to remove most of the early “always play MID” behaviour in the test bot without weakening the rule that MID contributes both stats. This is a **prototype heuristic, not a locked economy**.

## Rough simulation observations

These simulations use adapted V7 development-card stats, deterministic draw orders across many seeds and a crude threshold-aware bot. They are useful for failure detection, not balance targets.

### Directly copying V7 economy

With V7-like costs, too many persistent cards are deployed too early. Full end-of-period rescoring then compounds those stats across four scoring windows and produces very large scores. MID also becomes the default deployment because existing midfield cards were not costed for `ATT + DEF` contribution.

Conclusion: do not judge period banking using untouched V7 costs.

### Re-costing by natural zone power

Re-costing the same sample cards around natural zone power substantially improves placement diversity. In the better prototype runs, DEF / MID / ATT deployments become much closer to one another instead of MID dominating by default.

### Energy curves worth playtesting

The absolute Energy number is not important until costs are final. Two useful comparison curves emerged:

- `4 / 6 / 7 / 9`: roughly six player cards deployed per team in the crude bot; near-even zone use; lower late explosion.
- `4 / 6 / 8 / 10`: slightly more deployment and a much larger final-period spike.

The first produced roughly 8–9 combined goals in the crude action-enabled simulation; the second roughly 10–11. **These are not realism targets.** The key playtest question is whether the extra final-period scoring creates a satisfying comeback / explosion or makes earlier periods feel irrelevant.

### Manager timing

A generic unconditional `+5 ATT this period` manager is structurally dangerous because `+5` is exactly one goal band. A threshold-aware bot tends to cash that immediately whenever it guarantees a goal.

The prototype should instead use manager-specific effects whose value depends on board state, player count, chance types or matchup. A test effect scaling with players already in the chosen zone naturally encouraged later timing and removed the automatic period-one button problem.

### Chance cards

Typed Chances worked better as generated hand resources than as an independent chance bar in the rough model:

- they compete with players / Manager for Energy;
- a creator played now sets up a later period;
- holding a Cross until a receiver arrives is a real decision;
- they can produce threshold swings without changing the core goal formula;
- receiver and defender Actions can remain type-specific.

The exact default Cross / Through Ball values are deliberately not locked yet.

## Main unresolved test: period banking

The current engine intentionally uses the user's preferred rule: resolve and bank goals from the full board at the end of **every** period.

Its known consequence is tempo compounding. If a +5 attacking advantage remains unanswered for several periods, it can score repeatedly. Later periods also tend to contain more goals because more persistent cards are on the board.

That may be a feature: early cheap cards create tempo, the opponent must respond, and the final period can produce spectacular comebacks.

Do **not** replace this before a playable comparison. If playtesting says the same unchanged board scoring repeatedly is noisy rather than exciting, the first alternative to compare is “newly crossed goal bands” rather than changing the +5 goal rule itself.
